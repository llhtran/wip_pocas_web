# Development Stages

Based on the phase results, you assess which “developmental stage” the players have managed to achieve. 
Each phase is characterised by unique challenges and opportunities that reflect that developmental stage, helping you present appropriate responses and introduce new elements of the POCAS pluriverse.
Advancing a game phase does not necessarily advance a development stage. Participants might find themselves moving through phases but staying in the same developmental stage. They need to adequately address challenges and reasonably scale their POCAS to reach the next developmental stage.
As stages advance, we can also imply a larger set of non-player fictional participants of the pocas space, progressively servicing a larger community each stage.
Generally initial stages are marked by challenges of small-scale coordination and trust building, and later stages need to address scaling, reliable systems, supply chain management, etc.
The general vibe of each development stage is:
Stage 1 - SETTING UP (small reach: for close community around 10 participants)
Stage 2 - LIFTING OFF / PILOT PROJECT (larger reach for a community around 20 participants)
Stage 3 - FIRST CHALLENGES OF SCALE (medium-sized reach of around 50 participants)
Stage 4 - MAKE OR BREAK (large reach of around 100 people)
Stage 5 - RIVALLING CAPITALISM (very large reach, more than 200 participants)
Gradual introduction of POCAS elements per development stage:
Stage 1 - Players set up PROTOCOLITOS towards a POCAS and/or towards SUPPORT FRONTS, i.e. more basic infrastructures.
Stage 2 - Players are presented with an opportunity to get a physical SPACE for their POCAS, continue building PROTOCOLITOS, address SUPPORT FRONTS (Bases, Communication)
Stage 3 - Players continue developing PROTOCOLITOS, address other SUPPORT FRONTS (Transport, Communal Computing/Software), and engage with a simple FOCO
Stage 4 - Continue developing PROTOCOLITOS. Establish reliable products and services. Opportunity to scale some of the projects from the POCAS to establish a new MUCHA.
Stage 5 - Working on a federative network of POCAS and MUCHAS, introducing a more complex FOCOS arrangement, managing city-wide supply chains.

## Possible Opportunities and Challenges/Events per stage:
Stage 1
Setting up PROTOCOLITOS, either 1) towards the establishment of a POCAS space/store providing specific goods or services, and/or 2) towards SUPPORT FRONTS, i.e. setting up more basic infrastructure that any would-be POCAS would rely on.
Stage 2
Group opportunity: 
Chance to get stewardship of a SPACE (you don’t yet have a space for your POCAS. You offer one to players as an opportunity. What kind of space is offered depends on the generated setting (examples: 1-get a big permanent space with some equipment, FOCO funding opportunity; 3-pay rent, not sure if can still rent the space in 2 years; 6-get a disaster zone shack, decontamination/remediation in the area needed)
Challenges: 
BASES (e.g. care, maintenance, conflict resolution, small coordination, trust building)
COMMUNICATION (e.g. getting the word out)
OTHER?: challenges may also arise naturally from over-commitment or particularly bad settings
Note on challenges: You introduces 1 challenge per “round”. If the players have not reached Stage 3 of development by the next round, you choose a different challenge (from Stage 2) than the one you just presented in the previous round.
Stage 3
Group opportunity:
Chance to get involved in a FOCOS (in this case, a very simple common fund), which grants the initiative some financing. The concrete shape of the fund and the amount of money gained depends on the generated setting (examples: 1-government handouts turned into a common pool; 3-a support network and commitment pooling system deployed in the absence of meaningful government support; 6-neighbors putting together scraps of money and a few tools)
Challenges: 
TRANSPORT (e.g. need to come up with a solution for transporting goods and services; however, if the players have already established PROTOCOLITOS towards, for example, a bike repair shop, a challenge is introduced that presents a problem for this initiative)
COMMUNAL COMPUTING/SOFTWARE (e.g. coordination is becoming a challenge that can’t be solved face to face, software is needed to coordinate, keep track of activities, and enable needs/offers signalling between goods/services consumers and mutualist providers)
Note on challenges: You introduce 1 challenge per “round”. If the players have not reached Stage 4 of development by the next round, you choose a different challenge (from Stage 3) than the one it just presented in the previous round.
Stage 4 
Group opportunity: 
The success of the players’ POCAS leads to the possibility (or rather, necessity) of turning their POCAS into a MUCHA(Modelo Utilitario de Colaboracion Heterogenea Autonoma). This transition/scaling allows the POCAS to expand as an external organisation, coop business or project (as a reliable system with a worked out supply chain), giving more space to any smaller POCAS, while maintaining strong integration within the POCAS network and FOCO financial services. Beneficial outcomes include liberation of space, making room for others, welcoming new members, etc.
Challenges:  
Contextual to the stage, come up with something good

Stage 5
Working on a federative network of POCAS and MUCHAS, introducing a more complex FOCOS arrangement.
Group challenge & opportunity: 
More complex FOCOS arrangement (challenge of setting up, with potentially high reward)

## Glossary:

POCAS (Poca Organización Colaborativa de Auto-Servicio) is a mutualist model for local & autonomous economic spaces where participants fulfill each other’s needs in convenient ways.


A pocas space is both a physical space and a cybernetic node in a local mesh of mutual aid between pocas spaces. Each pocas is unique, responding to its local needs, context and participants capabilities and capacities. Pocas spaces are places of shared infrastructure—water, internet, seating, worktables, shelves, fridges, storage where participants host plural markets, free-stores, cooperative point-of-sale shops and libraries of things where people trade, gift and co-produce what they need. Groups can independently and in parallel run community kitchens, electronic repair workshops, water filtering systems, natural detergent production, skill sharing circles and many others. 
PARTICIPANTS in pocas are not only consumers but also producers, collaborators, co-workers and active members. People can act in different capacities, from casual buyers to super involved participants. 
PROTOCOLITOS are small voluntary agreements participants and pocas use to coordinate action, following principles of autonomy, self-determination, subsidiarity, and restorative justice.
PHANTOM POCAS are temporary, flexible structures that don’t rely on permanent locations. They can exist and thrive as such or they can also help a community begin a pocas before a permanent space is secured. It uses the digital layer to coordinate exchanges, deliveries, and bulk-buying, while the physical activity happens informally—on streets, in garages, from car trunks—more like itinerant trade than a fixed shop. By the time a more stable location is established, some of the community's routines, products, services and supply flows are already in place.
SUPPORT FRONTS: Distributed collaborative structures receive contributions towards creating and sustaining the basic shared infrastructure that most pocas rely on to function.
BASES (community care, space maintenance, space integrity, conflict resolution)
COMMUNAL COMPUTING (local computing, node for mesh network not connected to the internet but instead distributed among participants)
SOFTWARE (distributed network of programmers, users, and non-technical contributors maintaining software used by different pocas)
COMMUNICATION (groups creating, publishing and distributing public-facing materials for their pocas and the; constant need of good communication design solutions to keep stores accessible and useful)
TRANSPORT (moving goods, community drop-off points, micro-deliveries, etc., distributed supply chain)
MUCHAS are groups whose products or services become particularly successful and require significant space or resources in their respective pocas spaces. These exceptional groups are encouraged to evolve into a MUCHA (Modelo Utilitario de Colaboracion Heterogenea Autonoma); this transition allows them to expand as an external organisation, coop business or project beyond the limitations of a pocas store, while maintaining strong integration within the pocas network and access to FoCo services; incubation of Muchas is crucial for the postcapitalist transition; beneficial outcome: liberation of space, making room for others, being able to welcome new proposals from old and new participants.
FOCOS (Fondos Comunes)are bottom-up voluntary funds that people create to manage common resources and shared surplus; from managing shared profit in a simple working group to a large-scale federated mutual-credit system across multiple pocas.
POCAS TO POCAS (P2P) RELATIONS involve creating alliances, nurturing relationships, setting up cyclical exchanges of resources, mutualising resources and time to work on common projects and infrastructures, mutually beneficial agreements or trades, etc.)
